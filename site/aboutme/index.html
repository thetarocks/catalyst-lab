ABOUT ME / A RESEARCH PORTFOLIO

Learning by
making rules run.

I’m a high school student interested in computer science, economics and finance. I got drawn to mechanism design through its parallels with real-life politics: how rules shape representation, access and influence. Writing models gave me a way to make those connections concrete.

That is the thread between these projects. An admissions rule affects access to a scarce seat. A voting rule changes whose voice carries weight. A market’s matching rule determines the price someone actually pays. I want to make those rules easier to inspect and question.

Building the models has taught me to follow the intermediate state, not just the final chart. During development, the matching engine’s accepted list and an applicant’s own match state could disagree after a displacement. There is now a regression test that checks both sides. The tests, assumptions and source are linked so someone else can reproduce a result and challenge it.

Next, I’d like to learn from students and DAO participants about where the models miss their experience. I also want to test richer preferences, archive the governance ballots and add identity costs to the Sybil model. Those are next steps, not completed user studies.
