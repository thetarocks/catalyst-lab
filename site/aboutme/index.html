ABOUT ME / A RESEARCH PORTFOLIO

Learning by
making rules run.

I’m Sharvina Srivastava, a high school student interested in computer science, economics and finance. I got drawn to mechanism design through its parallels with real-life politics: how rules shape representation, access and influence. The JEE admissions race around me also made me curious about how fair counselling rules are. Models gave me a way to make those questions concrete.

That is the thread between these projects. An admissions rule affects access to a scarce seat. A voting rule changes whose voice carries weight. A market’s matching rule determines the price someone actually pays. I want to make those rules easier to inspect and question.

My starting point in DAO governance was quadratic voting. Editorial feedback on a rejected submission challenged the assumption that votes and capital concentration would stay fixed under a different rule. That led me to the Sybil follow-up. It taught me to ask how participants might respond to the mechanism, beyond recalculating its immediate outcome.

Next, I’d like to learn from students and DAO participants about where the models miss their experience. I also want to test richer preferences, archive the governance ballots and add identity costs to the Sybil model. Those are next steps, not completed user studies.

DAO / START → CHALLENGE → FOLLOW-UP

Reweighting is only the start.

Sharvina’s initial line of inquiry was quadratic voting. The preprint compared outcomes with observed votes held fixed. In editorial feedback on a rejected submission, an editor questioned the novelty of changed outcomes and noted that capital concentration could respond to new voting rules.

That objection prompted the Sybil follow-up: what happens when one balance is split among identities? The equal-split model isolates one response; it still does not model endogenous concentration or actual voter behaviour.

JoSAA / MOTIVATION → MODEL → BOUNDARY

Fair in which round?

Living in India amid the JEE admissions race made Sharvina curious about counselling fairness. The project connects matching theory to explicit quota rules and Freeze, Float and Slide decisions.

The distinction that matters: single-round DSIC is established theory; it does not settle the consequences of an irreversible action when later vacancies appear. The synthetic audit explores that narrower question, without claiming to validate official JoSAA outcomes.
